## The problem worth solving

A recording can preserve a lecture while leaving another audience unable to understand it. Theophonia works through Greek speech, specialist terminology and English translation to close that gap.

The difficult part is preserving meaning through several imperfect AI stages. A fluent sentence can still misrepresent the speaker. I built the workflow around finding and repairing those problems.

## What is working

The local archive contains **706 completed transcription and translation packages** and **369 completed review stages**, across 708 lecture manifests. These are recorded stage completions, not a claim that every lecture is approved for release.

**Evidence checked September 14, 2026.** The latest manifest in this snapshot was updated August 23. The manifests do not establish completed narration. Audio and final release remain separate gates.

## A repair you can trace

One recorded lecture repair changed three passages and marked the repaired result ready. It reused the existing transcription. The repair record binds the changes to the accepted source text.

That is the practical value: accepted work survives, and attention returns to the passages that need it. An unfinished stage stays visible instead of being mistaken for a finished lecture.

<details>
<summary>Engineering decisions</summary>

Speech detection checks coverage before translation. Reference retrieval helps correct specialist terms. Review compares Greek and English passages, with targeted repairs for supported findings.

The execution layer uses SQLite task state, stable passage identifiers and hashes that identify exact artifacts. Retries operate on individual stages. Source recording time and generated narration time remain separate.

I built the execution and quality layers around existing speech and language models. Listening checks and human release decisions remain necessary.

</details>

## Why this matters beyond lectures

Training archives, research interviews and specialist recordings face similar problems. This project shows how I turn a complicated process into inspectable software, with a clear place for human judgment.
