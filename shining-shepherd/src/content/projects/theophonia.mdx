## The engineering problem

Greek theological lectures contain names, scripture, liturgical phrases, and older forms of the language. Speech recognition can turn one unfamiliar term into plausible nonsense. A translation model may then make that error sound fluent in English.

I built Theophonia to prevent that chain from becoming an invisible failure. It treats every lecture as a set of versioned artifacts with passage level evidence, explicit quality states, and a release gate.

## How the pipeline works

The pipeline first creates a speech map with voice activity detection. Whisper large v3 transcribes the recording in windows, and a coverage check sends missed speech back for recovery before language work begins.

The Greek transcript is divided into stable passages. A terminology stage retrieves likely scripture, liturgy, and patristic references from indexed SQLite corpora. Those sources support post editing and translation without allowing retrieved text to silently replace the recording.

Greek and English then enter a structured review. Each finding points to a passage and its evidence. A supported finding can trigger a focused repair of that passage, including a new source window decode when needed. Accepted transcription outside the finding stays intact.

## Why the state model matters

Long AI pipelines fail in ordinary ways: a process stops, a model times out, a worker repeats a task, or an old artifact reaches a new stage. The execution layer uses a SQLite task ledger, leases, idempotent stages, local retries, and SHA 256 hashes that bind outputs to their inputs.

Execution status and quality disposition remain separate. A task can finish computing while its lecture remains blocked for review. The source recording clock also remains separate from generated narration timing, so subtitles and later audio cannot rewrite the original evidence.

## Evidence from the working archive

At the September 14, 2026 snapshot, the local archive contained **708 lecture manifests**, **706 completed transcription and translation packages**, and **369 completed review stages**.

One recorded repair provides a smaller proof. Review produced three supported findings in three passages. The repair changed those passages, reused the accepted transcription, and recorded the resulting quality disposition as ready. The system did not restart the lecture from the beginning.

These numbers describe completed stages, not public releases. Narration and final publication were still incomplete across the snapshot. Human listening and release approval remain required.

## What this demonstrates

Theophonia combines speech recognition, retrieval, translation, evaluation, workflow state, and failure recovery in one operating system. It is a concrete example of how I approach applied AI: preserve evidence, make failures visible, rerun the smallest safe unit, and keep a person at the final decision.
